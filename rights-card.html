📋 Freebie Rights Card

The cheat sheet for “can I use this free download?” — keep a copy next to your desk.

1) The four badges

BadgePlain-English meaning
commercial-okSafe for things you sell, even business or book work.
share-and-adaptSharing and editing allowed (usually with credit).
personal-use-onlyYour own projects only. Want commercial? Buy a license.
check-meRights unclear — read the license page first.

2) The licenses in one breath each

  • MIT — anything goes; just keep the copyright line in the file.
  • CC0 / public domain — yours for anything, no credit needed.
  • CC BY — anything, even commercial; give a credit line.
  • CC BY-SA — credit, and your remix carries the same license.
  • CC BY-NC — credit, but nothing commercial.
  • CC BY-ND — share as-is; don’t modify.
  • OFL 1.1 — font magic: embed, print, sell books with it; don’t sell the font itself.
  • “Personal use only” — the expensive-surprise clause; commercial costs extra.
  • Site terms (Freepik, Pixabay, etc.) — read the bullets: no reselling raw files, sometimes attribution, sometimes count limits.

3) The three questions at download time

  1. What can’t I do with this? (usually personal-only, no selling, no re-sharing)
  2. Who gets credit, and where?
  3. What do I keep? (license URL + file location → your catalog)

4) Attribution template

[Creator name] — [asset name] — [license, e.g. CC BY 4.0] — [link]

5) Red flags

  • “Free” with no license page at all
  • No commercial use, when commercial plans exist
  • “Contact for permissions” — fine, but ask before you build
